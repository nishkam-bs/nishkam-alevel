How each page works

Every knowledge organiser has the same layout, so you always know where to look.

Four content boxesThe knowledge for that topic, including any calculations and diagrams.
Exam trapA mistake that loses students marks, and how to avoid it.
Analysis chainA worked chain of reasoning you can adapt for any business.
Top grade connectionWhat the answer depends on - the starting point for your judgement.

How to use them

Reading a knowledge organiser is not the same as learning it. Use them actively. For quick recall, try the blurting page.

  1. Cover it and write it out

    Cover one box, write down everything you can remember on paper, then check. Add what you missed in a different colour. The gaps are what you revise next.

  2. Do the worked examples yourself

    Copy the numbers, close the PDF and work the answer out. Then check it, including the £, % or units. Change one number and do it again.

  3. Learn the exam trap

    Each page has a mistake examiners see every year. Explain to yourself, or a friend, why the wrong version is wrong.

  4. Write your own analysis chain

    Take the chain on the page and rewrite it for a different business - one from a past paper or one you know. Every step needs a reason, and the last step should link back to the question.

  5. Use the top grade box for judgement

    This box tells you what the answer depends on. Practise turning it into a one-paragraph conclusion that makes a clear decision.

  6. Little and often

    One or two pages a day works better than a whole unit the night before. Come back to the same page a week later and test yourself again.
