3.10.1 Managing Change — Lewin, K&S, Flexible Organisations

Lewin’s Force Field Analysis

Diagram: Lewin’s Force Field Analysis
How to Use Lewin in Exams
Step 1: Identify DRIVING forces from the case study (reasons change should happen).
Step 2: Identify RESTRAINING forces (barriers to change).
Step 3: Change succeeds when driving forces OUTWEIGH restraining forces.
AQA preference: Weakening restraining forces is generally more effective than strengthening driving forces — pushing harder creates more resistance.

Examiner Trap #1 — K&S Manipulation & Co-optation (2018 P1 Q12)
“A business gives a highly rewarded role in the change process to the trade union leader.” = manipulation & co-optation. Giving someone a symbolic role to neutralise opposition. NOT participation (not genuine input). NOT negotiation (no concessions exchanged).
Examiner Trap #2 — Delayering: Both TRUE (2023 P1 Q3, 2024 P1 Q3)
2023: S1 “increases span of control” (TRUE) + S2 “improves communication speed” (TRUE) = A.
2024: “Delayering is likely to...” = improve speed of vertical communication. Students who think delayering REDUCES span get this wrong — it does the opposite.
Examiner Trap #3 — Retrenchment & Resistance (2025 P1 Q8)
S1: “Less resistance if low union membership” = TRUE (less collective power).
S2: “Easier if high labour turnover” = TRUE (natural wastage reduces compulsory redundancies).
Answer: A — Both true.

Kotter & Schlesinger — How to Use Them in Exams

The Golden Rule
K&S is a two-part framework. Part 1 = WHY people resist (the four reasons). Part 2 = HOW to overcome resistance (the six methods). The examiner wants you to match the method to the reason. A student who picks the right method but cannot explain why it fits the specific type of resistance will not reach the top band.

Matching Methods to Reasons — The Exam Cheat Sheet

If the resistance is caused by…The best K&S method is…Because…
Misunderstanding / lack of trustEducation & communicationPeople resist because they don’t understand WHY. Explain the reasons clearly and the resistance dissolves.
Self-interest (fear of job loss, pay cut, status loss)Negotiation & agreementPeople resist because change threatens something PERSONAL. Offer something in return — pay protection, redeployment, enhanced redundancy.
Different assessments (genuine disagreement)Participation & involvementPeople resist because they think they know BETTER. Involve them in planning — their expertise may improve the plan, and involvement builds buy-in.
Low tolerance for change (anxiety, fear of the unknown)Facilitation & supportPeople resist because change itself is frightening. Provide training, counselling, time to adjust — address the emotional response.
Powerful opposition blocking progressManipulation & co-optationA key blocker is neutralised by being given a symbolic role in the change. Quick but ethically questionable.
Urgent change with entrenched resistanceExplicit & implicit coercionLast resort. “Accept the change or face consequences.” Fast but destroys trust and morale long-term.

How K&S Appears in Different Question Types

MCQ Questions (1 mark)
What the examiner asks: A scenario describing HOW a business handles resistance. You identify which of the six methods is being used.
The trap: Confusing manipulation with participation. Manipulation = token role to silence someone. Participation = genuine involvement in planning.
What to do: Ask yourself: “Is the person being given real input, or just being bought off?” Real input = participation. Token role = manipulation.
Tested: 2018 P1 Q12 (giving a trade union leader a role = manipulation).
Analyse Questions (9–12 marks)
What the examiner asks: “Analyse the barriers to change facing [business].” or “Analyse how [business] might overcome resistance.”
What to do:
1. Name the K&S reason explicitly. Don’t just say “employees are unhappy.” Say: “This represents self-interest as a reason for resistance (Kotter & Schlesinger) because workers fear losing their jobs.”
2. Use case study evidence. Quote specific details from the case study that prove this type of resistance exists.
3. Chain the analysis. Show WHY this resistance matters — how it will affect productivity, costs, morale, timelines, customer service.
4. Suggest the matching method. If the question asks HOW to overcome it, recommend the specific method and explain WHY it fits.
Tested: 2017 P3 (barriers to change), 2024 P2 (trade union consultation), 2025 P3 (Lewin/K&S combined).
Evaluate / Essay Questions (16–25 marks)
What the examiner asks: “To what extent can consultation overcome resistance to change for ALL businesses?” or “Evaluate whether education and communication is the most effective way to manage change.”
What to do:
1. Argue FOR the named method — explain when and why it works. Use a real-world or case study example. Chain the analysis to business outcomes.
2. Argue AGAINST — explain the limitations. When does this method FAIL? What type of resistance does it NOT address?
3. Evaluate using “depends on” factors: It depends on the TYPE of resistance (education won’t fix self-interest). It depends on the URGENCY (participation is slow). It depends on the POWER of those resisting (unions may require negotiation, not just communication). It depends on the SIZE of the business (participation works for 20 employees, not 20,000).
4. Conclude with a weighted judgement: “No single K&S method works in all situations. The most effective approach matches the method to the specific cause of resistance.”
Tested: 2024 P2 Q3.4 (16 marks — trade union consultation for all businesses).
K&S Combined with Lewin
When to combine them: Any question about managing change or overcoming barriers. K&S identifies the resistance and methods. Lewin identifies the forces. Together they give you a complete analytical framework.
How to combine: Use Lewin to categorise the forces (driving vs restraining), then use K&S to explain WHY the restraining forces exist (the four reasons) and HOW to weaken them (the six methods). This earns both breadth and depth marks.
Example sentence: “Employee resistance to relocation represents a significant restraining force (Lewin), driven primarily by self-interest as workers fear disruption to their personal lives (Kotter & Schlesinger). This could be weakened through negotiation — offering relocation packages and salary uplifts to offset the personal cost of moving.”

But an underperforming business needs speed: centralising decisions, cutting bureaucracy, one leader driving urgent turnaround. Think: new CEO takes charge and makes fast, decisive changes. The trap: students only know the “power to role as you grow” narrative.

Changing Culture — Why It’s Difficult

Problems of Culture Change
Time — takes years, not months.
Resistance — long-serving employees see change as a threat to identity and status.
Cost — retraining, restructuring, new leadership, new systems.
Inconsistency — if leadership says one thing but does another, employees see hypocrisy.
Loss of strengths — existing culture may have benefits (role = consistency; power = speed).

3.10.3 Strategic Implementation — Leadership, Communication & Network Analysis

Leadership & Communication in Strategy

Why Both Matter for Implementation
Leadership provides DIRECTION — deciding what change looks like, setting the pace, motivating others, removing barriers.
Communication provides UNDERSTANDING — ensuring all employees know WHY the strategy matters, WHAT their role is, and HOW progress will be measured.

Network Analysis

Key Terms — Know These Cold
EST (Earliest Start Time) = The earliest an activity can begin (longest path TO that node).
LFT (Latest Finish Time) = The latest an activity can finish WITHOUT delaying the project.
Critical path = The LONGEST route through the network. Any delay here delays the ENTIRE project.
Total float = Time an activity can be DELAYED without affecting project completion.
Float formula: Total float = LFT − EST − Duration. ZERO float = critical path activity.
Example: Network Diagram with Critical Path
Examiner Trap #6 — Float Calculation (2019 P1 Q12)
Total float = LFT (end node) − EST (start node) − Duration. The trap: students confuse EST and LFT, or subtract from the wrong node. Always use the LFT of the END node and EST of the START node for that activity.
Examiner Trap #7 — Delay Impact (2019 P1 Q13, 2024 P1 Q15)
Step 1: Is the delayed activity on the critical path?
If YES: Project delayed by the FULL delay. (2024 P1 Q15: Activity A on critical path, delayed 3 days = project delayed 3 days.)
If NO: Compare delay to activity’s float. Delay ≤ float = no project delay. Delay > float = project delayed by (delay − float).
Examiner Trap #8 — Network Statements (2022 P1 Q9)
S1: “Path with lowest total float is the critical path” = TRUE (critical path has zero float — the lowest).
S2: “At least one activity must have a float time” = FALSE (if all activities are on the critical path, ALL have zero float).
Answer: B — S1 true, S2 false.
Network Analysis Exam Technique
1. Forward pass FIRST: left to right, take HIGHEST EST arriving at each node.
2. Backward pass SECOND: right to left, take LOWEST LFT arriving at each node.
3. Critical path: where EST = LFT at a node. Or: the longest total duration path.
4. Float = LFT − EST − Duration. Zero float = critical.
5. If asked to amend a network, recalculate BOTH passes from scratch.
6. Always label clearly: EST and LFT at every node, duration on every arrow.

3.10.4 Why Strategies Fail — Strategic Drift, Planned vs Emergent, Contingency

Strategic Drift

Definition — AQA’s Favourite Strategic Concept
Strategic drift = Strategy becomes progressively LESS relevant to the changing external environment. The business continues as before while the market moves on.
Causes: Complacency, resistance to change, over-reliance on past success, dominant leader who refuses to adapt.
Examples: Kodak (invested in film while world went digital), Blockbuster (expanded stores while Netflix built streaming), Nokia (missed smartphones).
Examiner Trap #9 — Strategic Drift (Tested SIX times: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2024, 2025)
2025 P1 Q12: “Most likely to cause drift?” = High external change + Low strategic change.
2024 P1 Q23: 25-mark essay: “Is drift inevitable?” — No, businesses CAN adapt.
2022 P1 Q14: S1 “Emergent only from external” (F). S2 “Drift = changing strategy” (F). Both false = D.
2020 P1 Q6: Drift LEAST likely when willingness HIGH + external change HIGH = A.
2019 P1 Q7: Drift = “maintains a strategy no longer suited to current circumstances” = D.
2018 P1 Q14: S1 “Emergent = unplanned, arises from change” (T). S2 “Drift = strategy continually modified” (F). Answer = B.
The recurring trap: students confuse “drift” with “changing too much.” Drift = NOT changing enough.

Planned vs Emergent Strategy

Planned StrategyEmergent Strategy
DefinitionDeliberate, pre-determined, linked to objectivesDevelops through experimentation, learning, adaptation
ProcessAnalyse, Plan, Implement, ReviewAct, Learn, Adapt, Act again
AdvantagesClear direction; measurable targets; coordinatedFlexible; responsive; exploits unexpected opportunities
DisadvantagesRigid; time-consuming; can become irrelevantLacks direction; inconsistent; hard to coordinate
Best forStable environments; large businesses; capital-intensiveFast-changing environments; start-ups; crisis situations

Contingency Planning

Definition & Value
Contingency planning = Preparing for UNEXPECTED events with backup plans activated quickly in a crisis.
Value: Reduces response time; minimises damage; maintains stakeholder confidence; protects brand.
Limitations: Costly for plans that may never be used; can’t predict every scenario; may create false security.
Examiner Trap #10 — Competitor’s Planned Strategy is Unknowable
You can observe a competitor’s ACTIONS but CANNOT know their planned strategy (it’s confidential). Businesses can use their own data, economic forecasts, and market research — but not a competitor’s secret internal plans.

Essay 1: “To what extent is strategic planning more important than emergent strategy?” [24] — 2025 P3 Q6

Model Answer Guidance

Introduction — Standard Version

Strategic planning involves deliberate, pre-determined objectives with formal strategies linked to measurable targets. Emergent strategy develops organically through experimentation, learning and adaptation. Which is “more important” depends on the business environment, size, industry and competitive conditions, and one could argue that the most effective businesses blend both approaches rather than relying exclusively on either.

Introduction — PBA Challenge Version

P: One could argue that strategic planning is more important than emergent strategy because it provides the clear direction, measurable targets and coordinated resource allocation that businesses need to achieve long-term objectives. B: However, in volatile, fast-changing markets, rigid planning may become a liability, as businesses that fail to adapt risk strategic drift (Lewin’s Force Field Analysis). A: Ultimately, this essay will argue that the relative importance of each approach depends primarily on the rate of environmental change and the size of the business, with neither approach being universally superior.

Argument One (FOR Strategic Planning) — Version A: Explicit Chains of Analysis

One could argue that strategic planning is essential because a large PLC with thousands of employees cannot function without a clear plan, this means that departments must be aligned around shared objectives and resources must be allocated efficiently. Therefore, planning enables coordinated action across functional areas, as a result reducing the risk of conflicting departmental priorities and improving financial performance through measurable targets. This could lead to a reduced risk of strategic drift (Strategic Drift) by systematically analysing the external environment through SWOT analysis (SWOT Analysis), enabling the business to identify threats and opportunities before competitors. This can be explored through the success of Tesco PLC, which followed a deliberate market penetration strategy (Ansoff’s Matrix) after 2014 — closing unprofitable stores, reducing product range and investing in Clubcard data. This thus leads to the conclusion that planning provides the structural foundation upon which sustainable growth is built, particularly for large businesses in stable, regulated industries, meaning that strategic planning is more important than emergent strategy when predictability and coordination are paramount.

Evaluation of Argument One

However, this may not be the case if the business operates in a fast-changing environment such as the technology sector, where a 5-year plan could become obsolete within months. 2017 P1 Q12 — Lewin’s Force Field Analysis

According to Lewin’s force field analysis, which of the following is true?

A. Change occurs if driving forces are stronger than restraining forces  ✔
B. Change occurs if negative forces equal positive forces
C. A company grows if strengths and opportunities outweigh weaknesses and threats
D. A company grows if horizontal and vertical forces balance

Correct Answer: A

Why this is correct: Lewin’s model states that change happens when driving forces (pushing for change) overcome restraining forces (resisting change). If they are equal, the status quo remains. C describes SWOT analysis, not Lewin. D is not a recognised concept.

Examiner Trap
Don’t confuse Lewin with SWOT. Lewin = driving vs restraining forces. SWOT = internal strengths/weaknesses vs external opportunities/threats. They are different frameworks.
Strategy Tip
Lewin: driving forces > restraining forces = change occurs. Weakening restraining forces is often more effective than strengthening driving forces — pushing harder can create more resistance.

2017 P1 Q14 — Handy: Role Culture

Each employee has a job with clear rules and procedures. This corresponds to which of Handy’s cultures?

A. Person culture
B. Power culture
C. Role culture  ✔
D. Task culture

Correct Answer: C — Role culture

Why this is correct: Clear rules, procedures and defined job descriptions are the defining characteristics of role culture. It is the most bureaucratic and formal of Handy’s four cultures. Power = one boss decides. Task = project teams decide. Person = autonomous individuals.

Examiner Trap
Signal words for role culture: “rules,” “procedures,” “defined responsibilities,” “job descriptions.” If you see these, it is role — not task (which focuses on projects and teams).
Strategy Tip
Power = boss decides. Role = rules decide. Task = teams decide. Person = individuals decide. Learn these four one-liners.

2017 P1 Q15 — Network: Critical Path Duration

Using the network diagram, calculate the duration of the critical path.

A–D: Various durations calculated from the diagram.

Correct Answer: The LONGEST path through the network

Why this is correct: The critical path is always the longest route through the network — not the shortest. Add up the durations along each possible route from start to finish. The path with the greatest total is the critical path.

Examiner Trap
Students who pick the shortest path lose this mark every time. Critical = LONGEST = determines the minimum project duration.
Strategy Tip
List ALL possible routes from start to finish. Calculate total duration for each. The biggest total wins. This is the critical path.

2018 P1 Q12 — K&S: Manipulation & Co-optation

A business gives a highly rewarded role in the change process to a trade union leader. According to Kotter & Schlesinger, this is an example of:

A. Education and communication
B. Explicit coercion
C. Facilitation and support
D. Manipulation and co-optation  ✔

Correct Answer: D — Manipulation and co-optation

Why this is correct: Giving a specific person a desirable role to win their cooperation = co-optation, a form of manipulation. The underlying concerns are not genuinely addressed — the person is given a symbolic stake in the process to neutralise their opposition.

Examiner Trap
This is NOT participation (the role is not genuine input — it is a token designed to win cooperation). It is NOT negotiation (no concessions are exchanged). Co-optation specifically means giving a key resister a role in the change process.
Strategy Tip
K&S six methods in order: Education, Participation, Facilitation, Negotiation, Manipulation/Co-optation, Coercion. Know all six and when each applies.

2018 P1 Q14 — Emergent Strategy & Strategic Drift

Statement 1: ‘An emergent strategy is unplanned, arising in response to change.’
Statement 2: ‘Strategic drift occurs when a planned strategy is continually modified.’

A. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is true.
B. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false.  ✔
C. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is true.
D. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is false.

Correct Answer: B — S1 true, S2 false

Why this is correct: S1 is TRUE: emergent strategy is indeed an unplanned response to changing circumstances. S2 is FALSE: strategic drift occurs when a strategy is NOT modified despite changing circumstances. Continually modifying strategy is the opposite of drift — it is good management.

Examiner Trap
Drift = NOT modifying strategy. Modifying strategy in response to change = flexible adaptation (good). Drift = rigid failure to adapt (bad). Students who confuse these lose the mark.
Strategy Tip
Emergent strategy = flexible adaptation. Strategic drift = failure to adapt. They are opposites. Drift is always about NOT changing enough.

2019 P1 Q6 — Handy: Power to Role

A business changes its culture from decisions made by the original owner to stated rules and defined responsibilities. According to Handy, this is a change from:

A. Person to role
B. Person to task
C. Power to role  ✔
D. Power to task

Correct Answer: C — Power to role

Why this is correct: “Original owner making all decisions” = power culture (centralised, one dominant figure). “Stated rules and defined responsibilities” = role culture (bureaucratic, formal procedures). This is the classic growth transition: power → role.

Examiner Trap
Tested TWICE (2019 Q6 and 2025 Q9). Signal words: “clear procedures,” “defined responsibilities,” “rules” = ROLE. NOT task (= project teams). NOT person (= autonomous individuals).
Strategy Tip
Power → Role is the classic growth transition. As a business grows beyond the founder, it needs formal systems. This is the most commonly tested culture change direction.

2019 P1 Q7 — Strategic Drift

Strategic drift occurs when a business:

A. Implements a plan to cope with unexpected changes
B. Introduces an unplanned strategy in response to external changes
C. Introduces an unplanned strategy in response to internal changes
D. Maintains a strategy no longer suited to current circumstances  ✔

Correct Answer: D

Why this is correct: Strategic drift = continuing with a strategy that is no longer appropriate because the business environment has changed. The business has failed to adapt. A describes contingency planning. B and C describe emergent strategy.

Examiner Trap
Drift = FAILING to change. The business drifts away from what the market needs because it keeps doing the same thing. It is NOT about changing too often or too quickly.
Strategy Tip
Strategic drift: the gap between what the strategy delivers and what the market requires, caused by failure to adapt. Think Kodak, Blockbuster, Nokia.

2019 P1 Q12 — Network: Total Float

Using the network diagram, calculate the total float for Activity D.

A–D: Various float calculations.

Correct Answer: Apply LFT − EST − Duration

Why this is correct: Total float = LFT (of the end node) − EST (of the start node) − Duration of the activity. Use the LFT of the END node and EST of the START node for that specific activity. Zero float = critical path activity.

Examiner Trap
Students confuse EST and LFT, or subtract from the wrong node. Always use: LFT of the node the activity ARRIVES at, minus EST of the node the activity LEAVES from, minus the activity’s duration.
Strategy Tip
Float = LFT − EST − Duration. Write this formula at the top of your exam paper before you start. It prevents errors under pressure.

2019 P1 Q13 — Network: Delay Impact

If Activity B is delayed by 6 days, what happens to the duration of the critical path?

A. Does not change
B. Increases by 1 day
C. Increases by 5 days  ✔
D. Increases by 6 days

Correct Answer: C — Increases by 5 days

Why this is correct: Activity B has 1 day of float. A 6-day delay exceeds the float by 5 days. The critical path increases by the excess: 6 − 1 = 5 days. The first day of delay is absorbed by float; the remaining 5 days extend the project.

Examiner Trap
When a non-critical activity is delayed beyond its float, the project extends by (delay − float), NOT by the full delay. If delay ≤ float, there is no effect on the project at all.
Strategy Tip
Step 1: Is the activity on the critical path? If YES → project delayed by full amount. If NO → compare delay to float. Delay > float = project delayed by (delay − float).

2020 P1 Q6 — Strategic Drift: Least Likely

A table shows four scenarios combining managers’ willingness to change with likelihood of external changes. Strategic drift is LEAST likely in which scenario?

A. High willingness to change + High external change  ✔
B. High willingness + Low external change
C. Low willingness + High external change
D. Low willingness + Low external change

Correct Answer: A — High willingness + High external change

Why this is correct: Drift occurs when a business does not adapt to change. If managers are highly willing to change AND the external environment is changing significantly, the business is actively responding to real pressures. Drift is least likely because the gap between strategy and environment stays small.

Examiner Trap
Worst case for drift: low willingness + high change (Scenario C). The environment moves, but the business doesn’t. Best case: high willingness + high change — the business keeps pace. Note: the 2025 Q12 mirror asks for MOST likely (answer: high external change + low strategic change).
Strategy Tip
Strategic drift = gap between strategy and environment. High willingness to adapt keeps the gap closed. This question and the 2025 mirror are essentially the same concept tested from opposite directions.

2022 P1 Q9 — Network: Critical Path Statements

Statement 1: ‘The path with the lowest total float time is the critical path.’
Statement 2: ‘At least one activity in a network must have a float time.’

A. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is true.
B. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false.  ✔
C. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is true.
D. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is false.

Correct Answer: B — S1 true, S2 false

Why this is correct: S1 TRUE: the critical path has ZERO float — the lowest possible. All other paths have some float. S2 FALSE: it is possible for ALL activities to be on the critical path (if there is only one path through the network), meaning no activity has any float.

Examiner Trap
In a simple linear network (A→B→C with no parallel activities), every activity is critical and none has float. So it is NOT true that at least one activity must have float.
Strategy Tip
The critical path always has zero float. But not every network must have non-critical activities. If only one route exists, all activities are critical.

2022 P1 Q14 — Emergent Strategy & Strategic Drift

Statement 1: ‘An emergent strategy can only occur in response to external change.’
Statement 2: ‘Strategic drift occurs whenever a business changes a planned strategy.’

A. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is true.
B. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false.
C. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is true.
D. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is false.  ✔

Correct Answer: D — Both false

Why this is correct: S1 FALSE: emergent strategies can arise from INTERNAL or external changes (e.g., an employee’s idea leading to a new product). S2 FALSE: strategic drift is when a business FAILS to change its strategy despite changing circumstances — it is the opposite of changing strategy.

Examiner Trap
This is the fourth time AQA has tested strategic drift as an MCQ by 2022. The trap is always the same: drift = NOT changing when you should. Changing strategy is the CURE for drift, not the cause.
Strategy Tip
Emergent strategy = unplanned response to change (any change, internal or external). Strategic drift = failure to adapt (sticking with an outdated strategy). These are tested almost every year.

2023 P1 Q3 — Delayering

Statement 1: ‘Delayering increases the span of control of managers.’
Statement 2: ‘Delayering improves the speed of communication in a business.’

A. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is true.  ✔
B. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false.
C. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is true.
D. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is false.

Correct Answer: A — Both true

Why this is correct: Delayering removes management layers. S1 TRUE: fewer layers means each remaining manager oversees more people = wider span of control. S2 TRUE: fewer layers means fewer intermediaries for messages to pass through = faster vertical communication.

Examiner Trap
Students who think delayering REDUCES span of control get this wrong — it does the opposite. Removing a layer means the remaining managers must supervise more direct reports.
Strategy Tip
Delayering: wider spans, faster communication, fewer promotion opportunities, more autonomy for workers, BUT risk of manager overload. This is tested almost every year across multiple papers.

2024 P1 Q3 — Delayering Effects

Delayering in a business is likely to:

A. Decrease the span of control of managers
B. Improve the speed of vertical communication  ✔
C. Increase opportunities for promotion
D. Lead to less autonomy for workers

Correct Answer: B — Improve speed of vertical communication

Why this is correct: Fewer layers = fewer intermediaries = faster messages travelling up and down the hierarchy. A is false (span widens). C is false (fewer layers = fewer promotion opportunities). D is false (workers typically gain more autonomy with fewer supervisory layers).

Examiner Trap
This is essentially the same concept as 2023 Q3, tested from a different angle. AQA clearly considers delayering effects a core concept that students must know.
Strategy Tip
For any delayering question, the correct answer involves either “wider span of control” or “faster communication.” Eliminate the other options immediately.

2024 P1 Q14 — Network: Identify Non-Critical Activity

Referring to the network diagram, which activity is NOT on a critical path?

A. Activity B is not on a critical path.
B. Activity D is not on a critical path.  ✔
C. Activity G is not on a critical path.
D. All activities (A to G) are on a critical path.

Correct Answer: B — Activity D is not on the critical path

Why this is correct: Activity D has float time (it is not on the longest path through the network), so it is NOT on the critical path. Activities on the critical path have zero float — any delay extends the whole project.

Examiner Trap
Trace ALL possible paths. The activity that does not appear on the longest path is the non-critical one. Calculate float if unsure: float > 0 = not critical.
Strategy Tip
Critical path activities: zero float. Non-critical activities: positive float. If you can delay it without affecting the project end date, it is not on the critical path.

2024 P1 Q15 — Network: Delay on Critical Path Activity

If Activity A is delayed by 3 days, the duration of the critical path will:

A. Not change
B. Increase by 1 day
C. Increase by 2 days
D. Increase by 3 days  ✔

Correct Answer: D — Increase by 3 days

Why this is correct: Activity A is on the critical path and has zero float. Any delay to a critical path activity extends the entire project by the full amount of the delay. 3-day delay = 3-day increase in critical path duration.

Examiner Trap
Compare this to 2019 Q13 (Activity B delayed by 6 days, answer was 5 days because B had 1 day of float). When the activity IS on the critical path, there is no float to absorb the delay — the full delay hits the project.
Strategy Tip
Critical path delay = full project delay. Non-critical delay = check float first. This distinction is the entire logic of network analysis questions.

2025 P1 Q8 — Retrenchment & Resistance

Statement 1: ‘Retrenchment faces less resistance if trade union membership is low.’
Statement 2: ‘Retrenchment is easier if labour turnover is high.’

A. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is true.  ✔
B. Statement 1 is true. Statement 2 is false.
C. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is true.
D. Statement 1 is false. Statement 2 is false.

Correct Answer: A — Both true

Why this is correct: S1 TRUE: low union membership means less collective bargaining power and less organised resistance to redundancies. S2 TRUE: high labour turnover means employees are already leaving naturally (natural wastage), reducing the need for compulsory redundancies and therefore reducing resistance.

Examiner Trap
Both statements work through the same logic: factors that reduce the INTENSITY of resistance make retrenchment easier. Low unions = less collective power. High turnover = natural wastage does the work for you.
Strategy Tip
Link this to K&S: resistance to retrenchment is driven by self-interest (fear of job loss). Anything that reduces the number of people affected or their ability to organise collectively makes it easier.

Calculations Network Analysis Worked Examples

Total Float Calculation

Full Working

Formula: Total float = LFT (of end node) − EST (of start node) − Duration

Example: Activity D: EST at start node = 4, Duration = 3, LFT at end node = 12.

Calculation: Float = 12 − 4 − 3 = 5 weeks

Meaning: Activity D can be delayed by up to 5 weeks without affecting the project.

Impact of Delay on Critical Path

How to Approach Delay Questions

Step 1: Is the delayed activity on the critical path?

If YES: Project delayed by the FULL amount.

If NO: Check float. Delay ≤ float = no delay. Delay > float = project delayed by (delay − float).

Example 1: Activity A is on critical path. Delayed 3 days. Project delay = 3 days.

Example 2: Activity X has 3 weeks float. Delayed 5 weeks. Project delay = 5 − 3 = 2 weeks.

Example 3: Activity Y has 6 days float. Delayed 4 days. Project delay = 0 days (absorbed by float).

Amending a Network — Step-by-Step Visual Guide

Why Would a Network Need Amending?
In the real world, things go wrong. A supplier delivers late. A machine breaks down. Planning permission takes longer than expected. The council delays a safety inspection. When this happens, the duration of one or more activities changes, and the ENTIRE network needs recalculating — because one change can ripple through everything like dominoes.
The Scenario

A bakery is opening a new shop. The project has 5 activities (A to E). Activity C is “fit the kitchen equipment” — originally planned to take 4 weeks. But the oven supplier phones to say delivery is delayed. Activity C will now take 7 weeks instead of 4.

We need to work out: What does this do to the project? Does the critical path change? Does the project take longer? Do any float values change?

Step 1: The ORIGINAL Network (Before the Change)

Here is the original network. The critical path is A → C → E = 10 weeks. Activity B → D is a parallel path taking only 4 weeks, so B and D have float.

Original Network — Before Amendment
Original Summary

Path 1: A (3) + C (4) + E (3) = 10 weeks ← CRITICAL PATH (longest)

Path 2: B (2) + D (2) + E (3) = 7 weeks ← Not critical (3 weeks shorter)

Float for B: LFT (5) − EST (0) − Duration (2) = 3 weeks

Float for D: LFT (7) − EST (2) − Duration (2) = 3 weeks

Project duration: 10 weeks

Step 2: Make the Change — Activity C Goes from 4 to 7 Weeks

The oven supplier is 3 weeks late. Cross out the old duration (4) and write the new one (7). Now we need to redo ALL the maths from scratch.

Step 3: Redo the Forward Pass (Left to Right)

Forward Pass — Finding the New ESTs

Node 1: EST = 0 (always starts at 0). No change.

Node 2: Only Activity A arrives here. EST = 0 + 3 = 3. No change.

Node 3: Only Activity B arrives here. EST = 0 + 2 = 2. No change.

Node 4: Two activities arrive — C and D. C finishes at 3 + 7 = 10. D finishes at 2 + 2 = 4. Take the HIGHEST = 10. (Was 7 — this has CHANGED.)

Node 5: Only Activity E arrives. EST = 10 + 3 = 13. (Was 10 — this has CHANGED.)

New project duration = 13 weeks (was 10). The project is now 3 weeks longer.

Step 4: Redo the Backward Pass (Right to Left)

Backward Pass — Finding the New LFTs

Node 5: LFT = 13 (same as the new EST at the final node). Was 10 — CHANGED.

Node 4: Only Activity E leaves. LFT = 13 − 3 = 10. (Was 7 — CHANGED.)

Node 2: Only Activity C leaves. LFT = 10 − 7 = 3. No change (still 3).

Node 3: Only Activity D leaves. LFT = 10 − 2 = 8. (Was 5 — CHANGED.)

Node 1: Two activities leave — A and B. A needs: 3 − 3 = 0. B needs: 8 − 2 = 6. Take the LOWEST = 0. No change.

Key Takeaways — What Changed and Why
1. ONE activity changed — but THREE nodes had their numbers changed. One domino knocks over the others.
2. The project got 3 weeks longer (from 10 to 13) because C was on the critical path. If C had been on the non-critical path instead, the project might not have changed at all.
3. The float for B and D ALSO changed — even though nothing about B or D was different. This is because float depends on the LFT of the node they finish at, and THOSE changed.
4. The critical path stayed the same route (A → C → E) this time. But if the change had been big enough to make Path 2 longer than Path 1, the critical path would have SWITCHED to B → D → E. Always check.
5. When the exam says “amend the network,” it means: change the number, redo the forward pass, redo the backward pass, recalculate float, check if the critical path changed.
Common Mistakes on Amendment Questions
1. Only recalculating the changed activity — you must redo BOTH passes for the entire network.
2. Assuming the critical path stays the same — it may shift to a different route entirely.
3. Forgetting that float values change for ALL activities, not just the amended one.
4. Not checking whether a previously non-critical path has become critical due to the change.
