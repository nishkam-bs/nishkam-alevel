Exam technique

What separates Level 3 from Level 2 on 9-mark and 12-mark questions.

The structure of a 9-marker

A 9-mark question tests three things equally: Knowledge (AO1) — do you understand the concept? Application (AO2) — can you use it in the context given? Analysis (AO3) — can you build a chain of reasoning from the concept to the outcome the question asks about?

You do not need a conclusion. You do not need evaluation. You need depth — one well-developed argument with 5-7 causal links is better than two shallow ones with 2-3 links each.

Start points and end points

Every 9-marker has a start point (the concept) and an end point (the outcome). Your job is to build a chain between them.

If the question asks about profit and your chain stops at "sales increase" — you are not finished. You need to explain what happens to profit. Every chain must reach the exact endpoint the question asks about.

Chain depth

This is the single biggest discriminator between Level 2 and Level 3.

Level 2 (2-3 links)Delayering reduces costs. This increases profit.
Level 3 (5-7 links)Delayering removes a layer of management, which widens the span of control for remaining managers. This means each manager oversees more employees, which could speed up communication because there are fewer layers for decisions to pass through. Faster decision-making may allow the business to respond more quickly to changes in customer demand, which could help it retain customers and maintain revenue. If the salary savings from the removed management layer exceed any short-term disruption costs, this could increase the business's operating profit.

Models in brackets

When you use a model or theory, weave it into the chain rather than describing it separately. Write the model name in brackets mid-sentence:

"...which could satisfy employees' need for recognition and achievement (Maslow — esteem needs), reducing their desire to leave..."
"Maslow's hierarchy has five levels. The first level is physiological needs..." (This is model dumping. It does not earn AO3 marks.)

Paper 3 Q1/Q2 (12 marks)

These work the same way as 9-markers, but with case study context. The examiner confirmed in 2026 that one fully developed argument can earn 12/12. You do not need two arguments. Depth beats breadth.

Use the insert data throughout your chain. Quote numbers. Reference specific details about the business. This is where your AO2 marks come from.

Errors that cost marks nationally

HerzbergPay is a hygiene factor, not a motivator. Claiming pay motivates employees is a conceptual error.
MaslowListing five levels is not enough. You must explain the hierarchical mechanism — why satisfying one level leads to the next.
Debt factoringSignificant national knowledge gap. Make sure you can explain the mechanism, not just the definition.
Question driftRe-read the question before each paragraph. If your chain is heading towards "sales" but the question asks about "labour turnover", you are drifting.
Assertion"This will increase profit" is not analysis. You need to explain WHY and HOW, step by step.
Wrong endpointAnalysing to "motivation" when the question asks about "profit" is an incomplete chain. Always check what the question actually asks.
