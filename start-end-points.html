Q18 Analyse how an understanding of Maslow's theory of motivation might help a business to lower its labour turnover. 9 marks
Maslow's hierarchy → identify unmet needs → targeted HR policies → greater job satisfaction → lower labour turnover
StartMaslow's hierarchy of needs — five levels from physiological to self-actualisation. Each level must be substantially met before the next motivates.
EndLower labour turnover — fewer staff leaving as a proportion of the workforce. The question is asking you to connect motivational theory to retention.
ChainIf a business understands which level employees are at, it can target the right need. E.g. if pay already meets physiological/safety needs, then improving social needs (teamwork, belonging) or esteem needs (recognition, responsibility) becomes the priority → employees feel more valued → less likely to seek employment elsewhere → labour turnover falls → saves recruitment and training costs.
Q19 A business has operated with poor cash flow and low profit levels for a number of years. It decides to use debt factoring as a source of finance. Analyse the possible impact of this decision on its cash flow and profit level. 9 marks
Debt factoring → immediate cash from receivables → improved cash flow → but factoring fee reduces revenue received → cash flow vs profit trade-off
StartDebt factoring — selling receivables (money owed by customers) to a factor at a discount in exchange for immediate cash.
EndCash flow and profit level — you must address BOTH. Cash flow improves immediately but profit may fall due to the factoring cost.
ChainFactoring converts receivables into immediate cash → cash flow improves → business can pay suppliers on time / avoid overdraft charges → BUT the factor takes a percentage (often 2–5%) so total revenue received falls → this reduces profit margin. Also, reliance on factoring may signal financial weakness to stakeholders.

[Network diagram provided with activities: Recruit staff, Staff induction, Staff training, Set up factory layout, Install machinery] 9 marks
Critical path / HR activities → HR activities on critical path → reduce duration of HR tasks → shorter critical path / faster project completion
StartCritical path analysis and HR function — the critical path has zero float. HR activities (recruit staff → induction → training) are ON the critical path, meaning delays to them delay the whole project.
EndReduced length of the critical path — the total project duration falls, meaning the business completes faster and can start generating revenue sooner.
ChainThe network shows HR activities (A: Recruit staff 9 weeks → B: Induction 1 week → C: Training 5 weeks = 15 weeks) are on the critical path. To shorten it, focus on reducing these durations — e.g. faster recruitment (use agencies), condensed induction, or more intensive training → this directly reduces the project duration because these are critical activities with zero float → earlier completion → earlier revenue generation.

Q1 Using Lewin's force field analysis, analyse the case for VBM closing its EU marketing offices and centralising marketing in Vietnam. 12 marks
Context: VBM is retrenching from EU markets and consolidating operations to its Vietnam base.
Lewin's force field analysis→ driving forces vs restraining forces→ case for centralising marketing in Vietnam
StartLewin's force field analysis — weighing driving forces (pushing for change) against restraining forces (resisting change). Change happens when driving > restraining.
EndWhether VBM should close EU offices — build two developed chains showing driving forces outweigh restraining forces (or vice versa).
ChainDriving forces: cost savings from closing expensive EU offices → simpler structure → aligns with retrenchment strategy.
